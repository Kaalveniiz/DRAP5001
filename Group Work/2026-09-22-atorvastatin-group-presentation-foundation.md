## Page 4 — Raw Material Risk Assessment

### 1. What to put on the slide

**Slide title: Raw Material Risk Assessment**

**Product:** Atorvastatin tablets, **20 mg, immediate release**.

**Materials and potential risks:**

| Material | Material variation / concern | Potential effect on product quality | Preliminary risk | Reason for risk level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Atorvastatin calcium (API) | Particle-size distribution shifts | Altered dissolution | **High** | Meaningful lot variation is assumed possible despite acceptable identity / purity; significant dissolution impact is plausible. |
| Atorvastatin calcium (API) | Solid form / crystalline–amorphous content differs | Altered dissolution | **Medium** | Specifying and verifying the intended crystalline form is assumed to reduce the likelihood of a substantial form difference. |
| API-contact excipients, including croscarmellose sodium | Acidic surface properties | Potential lactone formation → increased degradation impurities | **Medium — tentative** | Credible degradation concern; likelihood in our complete formulation is unresolved. Medium is a low-confidence classroom judgment. |
| Croscarmellose sodium | Reduced swelling performance across grades / suppliers | Slower disintegration → possible slower dissolution | **Medium** | A fixed grade with reasonably consistent swelling is assumed to limit large performance changes; residual lot variation remains. |
| Magnesium stearate | Higher specific surface area | Possible slower dissolution | **Medium** | A fixed grade, amount, and mixing procedure are assumed to limit variation; the dissolution effect remains formulation-dependent. |

*Preliminary qualitative ratings under assumed material controls; these are our judgments, not published ratings. Effects and ratings require formulation-specific confirmation.*

### 2. Explanation for the slide — short speaking notes

**Opening:** These five rows show how raw-material properties could affect dissolution or degradation impurities. Published evidence supports the possible mechanisms; our preliminary ratings reflect the potential consequences and assumed likelihood under the stated material controls.

*The paragraphs below are the spoken explanation. Source links are for reference, not part of the script.*

#### Row 1 — API particle-size distribution → dissolution

API particle size affects the surface available for dissolution, so variation between batches could change drug release. We would compare tablet dissolution across a suitable particle-size range while keeping solid form and other factors consistent.

Source: [Kim et al. (2008), abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpb.2008.01.007). Particle size and solid-state effects were not isolated from each other.

#### Row 2 — API solid form → dissolution

Solid form describes how the drug is organised inside its particles. Crystalline and amorphous atorvastatin can dissolve differently, so we would verify the incoming API's solid form and assess relevant differences in tablet dissolution.

Source: [Shete et al. (2010), Table I and “Intrinsic Dissolution Rate”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2902319/). Not every solid-form difference produced a measurable dissolution change.

#### Row 3 — Excipient surface acidity → degradation impurities

Acidic excipient surfaces may promote atorvastatin lactone formation, increasing degradation impurities during storage. The supporting study used amorphous API in binary mixtures, so this flags a compatibility concern to test in our trihydrate formulation; it does not establish that croscarmellose is unsuitable.

Source: [Govindarajan et al. (2015), Table I and “Solid-State Stability”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4370965/). Tested under accelerated storage conditions.

#### Row 4 — Croscarmellose swelling performance → disintegration and dissolution

Croscarmellose takes up water and swells to help the tablet break apart. Grades with weaker swelling may slow disintegration and potentially dissolution, so we would compare both using candidate grades at the same ingredient amount.

Source: [Zhao & Augsburger (2006), abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16749528/). Model-tablet evidence; the dissolution effect in our atorvastatin formulation remains to be tested.

#### Row 5 — Magnesium stearate surface area → dissolution

Magnesium stearate grades can behave differently even at the same amount. Higher surface area was associated with slower dissolution in a model formulation, so we would compare candidate grades while keeping lubricant amount and mixing conditions constant.

Source: [Calahan et al. (2021), abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33267700/). The reported effect requires confirmation in our formulation.

### 3. Supporting background — for preparation and questions

The API appears twice because particle size and solid form are different attributes. Croscarmellose appears in both chemical-compatibility and disintegrant-performance relationships. Omission of other ingredients does not establish that they are low risk. Our proposed API remains calcium trihydrate.

**Assumed assessment basis:** Qualified suppliers, pharmaceutical-grade materials, a specified crystalline atorvastatin calcium trihydrate form verified on receipt, and fixed excipient grades. For this classroom scenario, croscarmellose swelling performance is assumed reasonably consistent, and magnesium stearate amount and mixing procedure are held constant. Meaningful API particle-size variation between lots remains possible. These assumptions do not establish actual material specifications, compatibility, or formulation robustness.

**How to interpret the ratings:** High identifies a credible significant quality concern requiring priority investigation. Medium identifies a meaningful concern whose impact is more conditional under the assumed controls. Medium does not mean that a resulting quality failure would be unimportant. Supplier qualification or a fixed grade alone does not demonstrate functional consistency; the assumptions require verification. Risk considers both consequences and likelihood, with uncertainty made explicit. [ICH Q9(R1), §4.3](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d77ad692-97ae-4a5f-acfa-e853193ef6aa_en?filename=mp_ich_q9guideline-ra_en.pdf)

**Surface-acidity qualification:** This row has the weakest basis for distinguishing Medium from High. Evidence from amorphous API in binary mixtures establishes a concern but does not establish a lower likelihood in our complete crystalline-trihydrate formulation. The tentative Medium rating must be reviewed using compatibility and stability studies. Calcium carbonate is not credited with reducing this risk until its protective effect is demonstrated.

#### What this assessment examines

The previous presentation prioritised finished-product quality attributes. This assessment examines how variation in an input material could affect those attributes:

> Material → relevant property → variation → effect on a finished-product CQA.

For example, atorvastatin calcium trihydrate is the **material**, its particle-size distribution is a **material attribute**, and finished-tablet dissolution is the **CQA potentially affected**. The actual effect requires evidence for the formulation. ICH Q8(R2), Annex §2.3, describes this connection between material attributes and drug-product CQAs. [ICH Q8(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/Q8_R2_Guideline.pdf)

#### Why these materials are being considered

The proposed product is a generic, immediate-release, film-coated atorvastatin tablet. The materials under consideration remain:

- **API:** Atorvastatin calcium trihydrate.
- **Proposed core excipients:** Microcrystalline cellulose (MCC), lactose monohydrate, hydroxypropyl cellulose (HPC), croscarmellose sodium, magnesium stearate, calcium carbonate, and polysorbate 80.
- **Proposed film coat:** An immediate-release hypromellose (HPMC)-based coating system.

The **API** provides the intended therapeutic effect. **Excipients** help manufacture the tablet and support its performance. The labelled 20 mg refers to atorvastatin content, not the total tablet weight or the mass of calcium trihydrate salt.

| Proposed material | Function | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| MCC | Filler and compression aid | Provides bulk and helps powder form a coherent tablet under pressure. |
| Lactose monohydrate | Additional filler | Adds bulk alongside MCC; the usefulness and proportions of the combination require development. |
| HPC | Binder | Helps particles adhere so the tablet withstands handling. A suitable binder grade is needed; a separate binder may not be necessary if the formulation already has sufficient strength. |
| Croscarmellose sodium | Disintegrant | Helps the tablet break apart when fluid enters it. |
| Magnesium stearate | Lubricant | Reduces friction when the compressed tablet is pushed out of the manufacturing die. |
| Calcium carbonate | Alkalising / stability-support candidate | May help create a less acidic local environment around the API. Its protective effect must be demonstrated in the formulation. |
| Polysorbate 80 | Wetting agent | Helps liquid spread over and contact particles; its effect on dissolution must be evaluated. |
| HPMC-based coating system | Immediate-release film coat | Forms a thin outer layer. An ordinary film coat does not automatically provide delayed release or a proven moisture barrier. |

These candidates have precedent in the [Lipitor ingredient list, §11](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a60cc18b-0631-4cf0-b021-9f52224ece65). Their proposed core functions are also described in [an atorvastatin-containing formulation patent, paragraphs 0105–0110](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/97/09/24/c30d874cfd4e12/US20140248345A1.pdf); that different product does not validate our formulation. [HPMC coating information](https://www.roquette.com/health-pharma/challenges/consumer-friendly-coating-solutions) supports the proposed immediate-release coating function.

#### Breakup, wetting, and dissolution

- **Disintegration:** The tablet breaks into smaller pieces or particles.
- **Wetting:** Fluid spreads over and contacts the material.
- **Dissolution:** Drug molecules leave the solid particles and enter solution.

A tablet can break apart quickly while its drug still dissolves slowly. The disintegrant and wetting agent therefore serve different functions, and neither substitutes for evaluating finished-tablet dissolution.

#### How to interpret the proposed list

The list is a development starting point, not a demonstrated optimal formulation. A material is not automatically critical simply because it is included. Its relevant properties and their effects on CQAs must be assessed before assigning risk ratings.

Atorvastatin–excipient compatibility also requires attention. A [surface-acidity study, Table I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4370965/) reported lactone formation with several tested excipients, including croscarmellose sodium. It used amorphous atorvastatin calcium in binary mixtures under accelerated conditions, so its results identify a concern to investigate rather than establish incompatibility in our complete trihydrate formulation.

The assessment must distinguish **material attributes**, such as particle-size distribution, from **formulation variables**, such as ingredient amount, and **process parameters**, such as mixing duration. No actual excipient quantities, material grades, or manufacturing route have been fixed. The risk ratings above are provisional judgments using the stated classroom assumptions.

| Parameter | Potential CQA impact | Preliminary risk | Reason for risk level | Proposed study |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Source footer:** FDA immediate-release tablet QbD example, Table 45 and §2.3.5 (different product; supports parameter selection).

### 2. Explanation for the slide — short speaking notes

**Opening:** This table focuses only on tablet compression, from feeding the prepared blend into the press to forming the tablet. Blending, any granulation or drying, film coating, and packaging require separate assessments.

#### Row 1 — Main compression force

We rate main compression force high because unsuitable force can change tablet structure enough to impair dissolution. Routine weight and appearance checks do not establish acceptable drug release, so we would study a suitable force range.

#### Row 2 — Pre-compression force

We provisionally rate pre-compression medium because we assume adequate air removal at baseline and no strong tendency for the blend to cap or laminate. Unsuitable settings can still cause serious integrity defects, so screening must test that assumption and trigger reassessment if defects occur.

#### Row 3 — Tablet press speed

We rate press speed high because running too fast for the material can impair filling and compression, affecting dose uniformity or dissolution. These failures are plausible across development settings, so we would study speed together with compression force.

#### Row 4 — Feed-frame paddle speed

We initially rate paddle speed high under the assumption that our blend may be susceptible to feeding variation or additional lubrication. These could affect dose uniformity or dissolution even when average weight is acceptable; screening may support reducing the rating.

### 3. Supporting reference and drafting decisions

**Assumed baseline:** Conventional rotary-tablet compression of the proposed magnesium-stearate-containing formulation, with acceptable incoming materials, a uniform starting blend, calibrated equipment, trained operators, and routine weight and appearance checks. Product-specific operating ranges and robustness have not yet been established. Material variability within incoming specifications remains possible, and initial blend uniformity does not guarantee absence of segregation during feeding.

**Row-specific assumptions:** For pre-compression, assume adequate air removal at a reasonable starting setting and no strong intrinsic capping / lamination tendency. This is the basis for the lower assumed likelihood behind Medium; it is not a finding from our experiments. For feed-frame speed, assume susceptibility to feeding variation or additional lubrication is plausible enough to prioritise screening; magnesium stearate's presence alone does not establish this susceptibility. The High ratings concern plausible failures across candidate development settings, not an assertion that failures are frequent at a suitable operating point. Weight / appearance checks provide limited evidence: acceptable average weight does not establish individual dose uniformity or dissolution.

**Assessment convention — qualitative development risk:**

- **High:** A significant CQA failure is reasonably plausible across unsuitable development settings; prioritise investigation.
- **Medium:** A meaningful failure mechanism exists, but its quality impact is more conditional under the assumed controls; conduct targeted screening.
- **Low:** Available knowledge supports a limited likelihood of significant impact within the stated conditions.

These are provisional judgements about quality consequences and qualitative likelihood, informed by analogous tablet experience. They are not measured failure probabilities, a numerical severity–occurrence matrix, or residual-risk ratings for a validated commercial process. Missing information alone does not justify “High.” The medium rating for pre-compression is particularly dependent on the assumed scenario and must be revisited if defects are observed.

- [FDA, Quality by Design for ANDAs: An Example for Immediate-Release Dosage Forms, Table 45 and §2.3.5, printed pp. 81–83 (hosted copy)](https://www.pharmaexcipients.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Quality-by-Design-for-ANDAs.pdf#page=81).
- The example concerns acetriptan, not atorvastatin. It supports the plausibility of these initial ratings, but its numerical settings and experimental results are not adopted for our product. Our ratings use the assumptions and reasoning above.
- In the example, subsequent feed-frame screening found no effect on content uniformity or dissolution within its tested range; that demonstrates how an initial concern can be reduced by evidence rather than treated as a confirmed defect.
- [ICH Q9(R1), §4.3](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d77ad692-97ae-4a5f-acfa-e853193ef6aa_en?filename=mp_ich_q9guideline-ra_en.pdf) — supports risk assessment informed by scientific knowledge and explicit treatment of uncertainty.
- “Proposed study” describes planned investigation. It does not claim that a design space has been established or that all four parameters are already confirmed critical process parameters.
- Focusing on compression does not select the upstream manufacturing route: direct compression, dry granulation, and wet granulation remain undecided.

| Study role | Variable or condition | Proposed treatment |

*Proposed study roles for tablet compression only; screening may change the final factor selection. An established design space requires supporting evidence.*

### 2. Explanation for the slide — short speaking notes

We first screen the material attributes and supporting press settings. We then propose studying compression force and press speed together, holding the formulation, prepared blend, tooling, environment, and screened pre-compression and feed settings consistent.

We would measure dissolution and dosage-unit uniformity, supported by weight variation, hardness, friability, and visible defects. If moisture or another material attribute changes the force–speed relationship, we would include it as an experimental factor or conduct a linked robustness study. Results from one blend cannot establish performance across all future material variability.

### 3. Supporting reference and study boundaries

#### Proposed experimental decisions

1. **Screen first.** Characterise representative incoming materials and investigate feed-frame and pre-compression settings before selecting the main experiment. A high initial risk can receive screening first; this determines its experimental role rather than declaring it unimportant.
2. **Vary together.** If screening supports the two-factor starting plan, study low, middle, and high levels of compression force and press speed in all nine combinations, with repeated centre runs. Select numerical settings from suitable preliminary trials; the levels are not yet product specifications.
3. **Hold consistent initially.** Use the same formulation and prepared blend, tablet press and tooling, controlled environmental conditions, and pre-compression/feed settings supported by screening. Verify that the assumed input conditions remain consistent during the experiment.
4. **Evaluate combined outcomes.** Compare dissolution and dosage-unit uniformity with criteria predefined for the study, alongside tablet-integrity measurements. Evaluate factor effects and interactions to identify candidate acceptable combinations, then perform confirmatory runs. Numerical acceptance criteria still require a product-specific basis.
5. **Test material variability.** Confirm performance with relevant material lots or attribute ranges. If a material attribute changes the acceptable force–speed region, include it in an expanded design or a linked robustness study before generalising the conclusions. Bulk density and flowability may be measured responses to material variation rather than independently adjustable factors.

#### Worked example — inconsistent die filling during compression

*Explanation only; a proposed control example, not an observed batch result.*

“Suppose a check after increasing press speed finds tablets below the predefined individual-weight limit. This flags possible underfilling and a risk to dose uniformity. We would stop compression, segregate potentially affected tablets, investigate feeding and speed, and verify acceptable performance before restarting. Passing weight checks alone would not establish content uniformity.”

**Details for questions:**

- **What and when:** Weigh individual sampled tablets and review their variation at start-up, after relevant adjustments, and at justified intervals during compression. Record press and feeder settings alongside the results.
- **Decision rule:** Compare individual weights and their variation with predefined in-process limits justified during development. The labelled **20 mg is the atorvastatin dose, not total tablet weight**; numerical weight limits, sample sizes, and intervals remain to be established.
- **Action if a limit is breached:** Stop compression and initially segregate output since the last acceptable check, extending the affected period if the investigation warrants it. Investigate die filling, material flow, and settings; correct the cause within justified operating conditions. Document the deviation and refer affected tablets for quality assessment and disposition.
- **Verification:** Before restarting routine production, confirm acceptable filling and tablet checks under the predefined restart procedure. Restart acceptance does not release the segregated tablets. Appropriate content-uniformity testing remains necessary because segregation can change API concentration even at an acceptable tablet weight.

## Page 9 — Preliminary FMEA — Tablet Compression

### 1. What to put on the slide

**Slide title: Preliminary FMEA — Tablet Compression**

**Scope:** Tablet compression, including feeding material into the press.

**Instruction for slide preparation:** Use the three **MAIN** scenarios on the presentation slide. Keep A–D as alternatives or backup notes. Assign S/O/D scores only after defining the scoring scale and assumed existing controls.

“Main” means preferred for the presentation, not a confirmed risk ranking. These remain potential scenarios to assess for our formulation.

| Selection | Failure scenario | Possible cause | Potential quality effect | Proposed prevention | Proposed detection |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MAIN 1** | **Excessive tablet compaction** | Unsuitable main compression force | Reduced dissolution | Establish and control suitable force settings | Monitor tablet strength; test dissolution |
| **MAIN 2** | **Inconsistent die filling** | Poor flow or unsuitable press/feed settings | Variable tablet weight and dosage-unit uniformity | Establish and control suitable feeding and press settings | Monitor individual tablet weights; test dosage-unit uniformity |
| **MAIN 3** | **Excessive additional lubrication in the feed frame** | Unsuitable paddle speed or prolonged residence | Reduced dissolution | Establish and control suitable paddle speed and residence conditions | Test dissolution |

### 2. Backup scenarios — alternatives or discussion notes

| Selection | Failure scenario | Possible cause | Potential quality effect | Proposed prevention | Proposed detection |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Backup A | **Capping or lamination**—the tablet top separates or the tablet splits into layers | Trapped air or unsuitable compression conditions | Damaged tablets; potentially altered dissolution | Optimise and control pre-compression and compression conditions | Inspect tablets; assess friability and dissolution |
| Backup B | **Insufficient tablet strength** | Too little compression force for the material | Friable tablets that chip or lose material | Establish and control suitable compression force | Check tablet strength, friability, and damage |
| Backup C | **Blend segregation during feeding** | Components separate as material moves through the feed system | Different drug concentrations between tablets, even at similar tablet weights | Establish feeding conditions that minimise segregation; control material handling | Sample and test dosage-unit uniformity across the run |
| Backup D | **Inconsistent compaction caused by moisture variation** | Incoming blend moisture varies or changes during exposure | Variable tablet strength and potentially dissolution | Establish incoming moisture acceptance limits and exposure controls | Check blend moisture, tablet strength, and dissolution |

### 3. Explanation for the slide — short speaking notes

FMEA means Failure Modes and Effects Analysis. Here we connect three possible failures during compression to their causes, quality effects, and proposed controls: excessive compaction, inconsistent die filling, and excessive additional lubrication in the feed frame.

Prevention addresses the cause to reduce the chance of failure; detection checks for a problem or its quality effect. For example, controlling compression force helps prevent excessive compaction, while strength checks and dissolution testing help detect its effects. Tablet strength alone does not establish acceptable dissolution.

We would assess severity, occurrence, and detection difficulty using a defined scoring scale. The scores and risk priority numbers remain to be developed; the controls in this table are proposals, not evidence that these failures are already controlled.

**Backup discussion point:** Main 2 concerns how much material enters each die; Backup C concerns how much drug that material contains. Tablet-weight checks alone cannot establish that the blend has remained compositionally uniform.

### 4. Scoring notes and supporting references

- **S — Severity:** seriousness of the consequence if failure occurs.
- **O — Occurrence:** likelihood under the stated conditions and existing controls.
- **D — Detection:** difficulty of detecting the failure before release; in the proposed convention, a higher score means poorer detection.
- **RPN — Risk Priority Number:** S × O × D. Numerical scales and decision thresholds must be defined before scoring. The template's RPN of 30 and “Medium–High” label are not adopted for our product.
- Future proposed controls must not be credited as existing controls in the initial assessment. Any later residual-risk scores would require reassessment of the implemented controls and their effectiveness.
- [ICH Q9(R1), Annex I.2–I.3](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d77ad692-97ae-4a5f-acfa-e853193ef6aa_en?filename=mp_ich_q9guideline-ra_en.pdf) — failure-mode analysis and its extension to criticality assessment.
- [AHRQ scoring explanation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK43662/table/advances-davis_60.t1/?report=objectonly) — illustrates severity, likelihood, and detection scoring; its numerical rubric is not adopted here.
- [FDA tablet-development example, Table 45 (hosted copy)](https://www.pharmaexcipients.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Quality-by-Design-for-ANDAs.pdf#page=82) — supports the three main compression concerns and Backups A–B. Product-specific effects and controls require confirmation for our atorvastatin formulation.
- [Research on blend transfer and content uniformity](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28032521/) — supports considering Backup C; the studied formulation differs from ours.
- [MCC moisture–compaction research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17669609/) — supports Backup D as a hypothesis to investigate; its application to our complete formulation needs confirmation.
