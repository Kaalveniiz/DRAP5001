## Report draft

### Introduction

#### Company description

LipidArc Pharmaceuticals Ltd. is a fictional specialty pharmaceutical company founded in 2018 and headquartered in Hong Kong, with approximately 250 employees. It focuses on cardiovascular and metabolic diseases, with core capabilities in small-molecule development and oral solid dosage forms. Its intended regulatory markets are the United States, European Union, United Kingdom and China. The company’s operating scope covers API and tablet manufacturing, packaging, quality-control testing and release. The company’s departments include Research and Development, Manufacturing, Regulatory Affairs, and Clinical and Safety, alongside a Quality Unit responsible for QA, QC and the QMS. Atorvastatin calcium tablets, 20 mg, are among the company’s main marketed products.

#### Product description

Our company’s generic prescription atorvastatin calcium tablets contain atorvastatin calcium trihydrate equivalent to 20 mg of atorvastatin per tablet. It is presented as an immediate-release, film-coated oral tablet in packs of 30 tablets for home use, with or without meals. The product is intended to lower elevated blood lipids, including total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, apolipoprotein B and triglycerides. The intended population includes adults with primary hyperlipidaemia and eligible patients aged ten years and older with familial hypercholesterolaemia, subject to the indication-specific treatment conditions.[1]

This report uses our company’s atorvastatin calcium tablets to examine how Quality by Design (QbD) and a quality management system (QMS) support consistent product quality in manufacturing. It defines the product’s quality objectives and potential critical quality attributes, assesses material and process risks, and proposes studies and controls to address them. It then sets out the responsibilities, documentation and review processes needed to implement those controls and evaluate their effectiveness.

### 1. Product quality foundations

#### 1.1 Applicable requirements and quality standards

Our company’s quality requirements cover both atorvastatin calcium API production and finished-tablet manufacture. Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) provides the operational foundation for these activities. ICH Q7 guides API manufacturing, while US 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 provide the finished-product GMP benchmark used in this project. This benchmark informs the proposed controls for facilities, personnel, materials, production, laboratory testing, records and batch release.[2,3] For the company’s intended EU, UK and Chinese markets, the corresponding regional requirements should also be addressed.

Our company manufactures atorvastatin calcium API for use in its own tablets and does not market the API separately. This report focuses on finished-tablet manufacture, including the quality of the API used, without examining the API manufacturing process. For this US-focused assessment, the USP–NF monograph **Atorvastatin Calcium Tablets** provides the compendial basis for the finished product, while **Atorvastatin Calcium** provides the corresponding basis for the API entering tablet manufacture. Both monographs must be read with the applicable General Notices and general chapters.[4,5] These standards inform the tests, methods and acceptance criteria for the API and tablets, supplemented by product-specific requirements justified by development and stability evidence.

Alongside manufacturing quality controls, our company’s responsibilities for its marketed tablets include collecting and evaluating safety information and reporting adverse events in accordance with applicable requirements. These pharmacovigilance activities support continued monitoring of the product’s safety after marketing.[8]

The QbD and QMS approach connects these requirements to product development and lifecycle management. ICH Q8(R2) guides the definition of product-quality targets and the understanding of formulation and process factors. ICH Q9(R1) provides the framework for assessing and managing quality risks, while ICH Q10 connects development knowledge to monitoring, CAPA, change management and management review. ICH Q11 additionally informs development of the API manufacturing process.[9,10] Together, these frameworks guide how our company will establish and maintain the quality of its atorvastatin tablets, alongside applicable GMP and pharmacopoeial requirements.

#### 1.2 Quality Target Product Profile (QTPP)

The Quality Target Product Profile (QTPP) translates the intended use of our company’s atorvastatin tablets into the quality characteristics that development must deliver. It provides a starting point for selecting the attributes to investigate and control.[11] For this project, the profile combines the product’s dosage form and strength—an oral tablet containing 20 mg of atorvastatin—with targets for dose consistency, drug release, purity and stability. Table 1 summarises the product’s quality targets and their rationale, ordered from product characteristics to intended performance and maintenance of quality.

**Table 1. Proposed QTPP for LipidArc atorvastatin calcium tablets**

| QTPP element | Project target | Rationale | Related potential CQAs |
|:-------------|:---------------|:----------|:-----------------------|
| Dosage form, route and use | Immediate-release, film-coated oral tablet for home use | Retain the selected presentation and achieve suitable release from the swallowed tablet | Dissolution; microbial limits |
| Identity and strength | Atorvastatin calcium trihydrate equivalent to 20 mg atorvastatin per tablet | Deliver the intended active ingredient at the labelled strength | Identity; assay; uniformity of dosage units |
| Dose consistency | Consistent drug content among individual tablets | Support reliable dose delivery throughout each batch | Uniformity of dosage units; assay provides complementary evidence of labelled-strength accuracy |
| Drug release and clinical performance | Reproducible immediate release and bioequivalence to an appropriate reference product | Support the intended performance of the generic medicine | Dissolution; assay; uniformity of dosage units, alongside separate bioequivalence evidence |
| Purity | Acceptable impurity and degradation-product levels at release and throughout shelf-life | Limit unwanted chemical substances and deterioration | Related substances/degradation products; assay |
| Microbiological quality | Meet applicable requirements for a non-sterile oral tablet | Provide acceptable microbiological quality during the product’s intended use | Microbial limits |
| Stability, storage and packaging | Target shelf-life of 24 months at 20–25°C in qualified moisture-protective packaging; 30 tablets per box | Preserve product quality during storage and supply the agreed pack presentation | Water content; related substances/degradation products; assay; dissolution; microbial quality as relevant |

The 20 mg strength refers to the atorvastatin content of each tablet; the total tablet mass also includes excipients. The QTPP addresses both accuracy of drug content relative to the labelled strength and consistency among individual tablets. These objectives are assessed through assay and dosage-unit uniformity testing, respectively.[12]

The release and stability objectives guide the assessment of formulation, manufacturing and packaging factors that may affect tablet quality. The assessment considers consistent drug release and the maintenance of acceptable quality throughout the stated shelf-life, including the protection provided by the selected packaging.

#### 1.3 Potential critical quality attributes and their rationale

The quality targets in Table 1 provide the basis for identifying the potential critical quality attributes assessed in this section. Critical quality attributes are the measurable product properties that must remain within appropriate limits to achieve the intended quality. Potential CQAs can be identified from the QTPP, then refined as formulation and process understanding develops.[11] This assessment covers seven potential CQAs for our company’s atorvastatin tablets. Table 2 links the seven potential CQAs to the product targets and proposed assessment methods, following the sequence of the corresponding tests in ICH Q6A Sections 3.2.2 and 3.3.2.1.[12]

**Table 2. Potential finished-product CQAs and proposed assessment basis**

| Potential CQA | Quality target and consequence of failure | Proposed assessment basis |
|:--------------|:----------------------------------------|:--------------------------|
| Identity | An incorrect active ingredient would make the product unsuitable for its intended use; a failed identification test requires investigation.[14] | Appropriate finished-product identification testing under the applicable monograph/specification |
| Assay | Drug content must correspond to the labelled 20 mg strength; an incorrect amount could alter exposure | Quantitative assay against justified release and shelf-life criteria |
| Related substances and degradation products | Relevant chemical impurities must remain within justified limits; excessive levels can raise safety concerns, and degradation products may indicate deterioration | Finished-product testing for relevant degradation products and other impurities requiring control in tablets, informed by API impurity controls and applicable monograph requirements[12,15] |
| Dissolution | Drug release must support the intended immediate-release performance; slow or variable release could compromise that objective | Applicable dissolution requirements and product-specific development comparisons |
| Uniformity of dosage units | Individual tablets must deliver consistent amounts of drug; acceptable average content can conceal variation between units | Applicable pharmacopoeial dosage-unit uniformity procedure and acceptance criteria |
| Water content | Moisture must remain compatible with product stability and performance; variation is a potential contributor to degradation or altered tablet behaviour | A suitable water-content assessment, with limits justified by formulation and stability evidence |
| Microbial limits | The non-sterile tablet must have acceptable microbiological quality; contamination may make it unsuitable for use | Applicable pharmacopoeial microbiological requirements and a justified testing strategy |

Identity, assay and dosage-unit uniformity are retained separately because they measure different aspects of dose quality. Confirming the API’s identity on receipt does not complete the assessment of the finished tablet. Likewise, an acceptable assay result does not demonstrate that every tablet has acceptable drug content. The finished-product specification and sampling arrangements must address these distinct questions.[12]

Dissolution is the principal product-performance attribute in the existing case work. The material and process assessments identify API particle properties, excipient behaviour, lubrication and compression as factors to investigate for their possible effects on release. These are development hypotheses for LipidArc’s formulation. Passing a dissolution test alone will not be presented as proof of bioequivalence; the clinical-performance target requires its own justified evidence.

Impurity control distinguishes API synthesis impurities from degradation products formed through chemical changes during manufacture or storage, including interactions with excipients or the immediate container. The distinction concerns origin and control approach, rather than a strict division before and after production.[15] LipidArc will address synthesis impurities through API manufacturing controls and the applicable API specification, while investigating degradation through formulation, processing, packaging and stability studies. Synthesis impurities already controlled in the API and not also degradation products generally need not be retested in tablets; finished-product testing must cover relevant degradation products and other impurities required by the applicable specification.[12] These impurity and water-content assessments connect release quality to stability throughout shelf-life. Water content remains a potential CQA because the project has identified moisture-related stability as a development concern. Its final criticality, method and limits require supporting data. Microbiological quality similarly requires a justified assessment for this non-sterile product. Reduced or omitted routine microbial testing would require scientific support; it does not follow simply from assigning microbial risk a lower priority.[12]

Applicable pharmacopoeial requirements will anchor the specifications, supplemented by justified product-specific criteria where necessary. The table is therefore a rationale for the proposed attribute set, rather than a completed specification. The seven potential CQAs will guide the development work in Part 2, which will determine how material and process controls contribute to their achievement.

#### 1.4 Initial quality priorities and transition to development

LipidArc uses the later case-study labels as preliminary priorities for investigation and control. This interpretation follows the presentation’s supporting review notes; the labels do not represent a validated numerical ranking. Identity, impurity control and dissolution receive high priority. Identity is a fundamental safeguard against supplying the wrong active ingredient. Impurity control requires attention to both the incoming API and changes during tablet manufacture and storage. Dissolution receives particular development attention because the group’s material and process assessments identify several possible influences on drug release. These priorities help direct investigation and control effort; they are not evidence that failures have occurred in the proposed product.

The later case study assigns medium priority to assay, dosage-unit uniformity and water content, and a lower monitoring priority to microbial limits. The supporting notes explain the medium assay priority as an assumption of less unresolved development work because ingredient control and established testing offer a familiar approach. This does not demonstrate process capability or reduce the importance of correct strength. The medium dosage-unit uniformity priority is particularly tentative: mixing and feeding performance have not been demonstrated for the proposed formulation, and the recorded feeding concerns still require investigation. Water-content and microbial priorities likewise remain subject to formulation, process and stability evidence. All seven attributes require appropriate controls regardless of their initial priority label.

A formal risk assessment will define the failure scenario, evaluate its consequences and likelihood, and document uncertainty; detectability may also be considered when the chosen method requires it.[13] The preliminary CQA priorities are therefore a starting point for the next chapter’s material and process assessments. Part 2 will examine how API attributes, excipient properties and manufacturing conditions could affect the seven CQAs, identify the studies needed to test those relationships, and use the findings to develop the proposed control strategy.

### 2. QbD development and control strategy

#### 2.1 Proposed formulation and manufacturing approach

LipidArc will develop its 20 mg immediate-release atorvastatin tablet using wet granulation, aiming to produce granules that feed and compact consistently. Suitability depends on achieving these benefits while meeting dissolution and stability criteria. This chapter addresses finished-tablet manufacture in the US FDA context, beginning with atorvastatin calcium trihydrate as an input material. The formulation, studies and controls are proposals: ingredient quantities and grades, equipment, operating ranges and acceptance criteria require development evidence.

The proposed core contains microcrystalline cellulose (MCC) and lactose monohydrate as fillers, hydroxypropyl cellulose (HPC) as binder, croscarmellose sodium as disintegrant, polysorbate 80 as wetting agent and magnesium stearate as lubricant. Calcium carbonate is included as an alkalising component intended to support stability. This composition and the following addition sequence draw on a published atorvastatin patent; its numerical recipe and operating settings are not adopted.[16]

After acceptance, dispensing and screening, the API, calcium carbonate, MCC, lactose and part of the croscarmellose will be blended. Separately, polysorbate 80 will be incorporated into purified water, followed by HPC dispersion and hydration. This liquid will be added to the initial blend during wet granulation. The granules will be dried and milled or sieved, then blended with the remaining croscarmellose. Magnesium stearate lubrication will precede compression. The cores will receive an immediate-release hypromellose (HPMC)-based film coat and moisture-protective packaging in the agreed 30-tablet presentation.

The croscarmellose split leaves a portion unexposed to wet granulation; its effect on disintegration and dissolution will be tested. Late magnesium stearate addition allows separate control of lubricant mixing exposure.[21,22] Liquid exposure, drying and milling may alter granule properties and downstream performance. Reported processing-related solid-state changes in atorvastatin calcium trihydrate tablets support investigation, but a form change alone does not establish a quality failure.[17]

#### 2.2 Material risks affecting product quality

The assessment considers each failure's consequences and qualitative likelihood under stated conditions, including whether assumed controls prevent unsuitable material progressing. Following ICH Q9(R1), uncertainty is recorded through assumptions and evidence needs.[13] The baseline assumes identification, dispensing checks and suitable incoming chemical-quality testing; functional performance after wet processing remains unresolved.

The project defines investigation priorities as follows. **High** applies when a significant quality failure is scientifically plausible under anticipated variation and baseline checks do not adequately address the pathway. **Medium** applies when a consequential failure remains possible but a specified control or supported restriction makes progression to unacceptable tablets less likely. **Low** requires evidence of a minor consequence or unlikely failure under defined conditions. These are project criteria, not ICH-prescribed categories or measured probabilities. Missing information alone does not establish a rating, and planned controls are not credited as effective. Ratings will be reviewed as evidence develops; they do not replace specifications or establish acceptable residual risk.

**Table 3. Preliminary material and formulation risks**

| Attribute or factor | Potential quality failure | Priority and basis |
|:--------------------|:--------------------------|:-------------------|
| API particle-size distribution | Changed dissolution | **High:** Meaningful size variation is assumed possible despite acceptable identity and chemical quality |
| API solid-state form | Altered dissolution or degradation behaviour | **High:** Reported form-dependent dissolution and processing changes support investigation; incoming verification does not cover later transformation[17,18] |
| API purity and potency | Excess impurities or incorrect assay | **Medium, conditional:** Representative sampling, suitable methods and rejection are assumed to intercept unacceptable incoming API. Severity remains unchanged; later degradation is assessed separately |
| API and excipient water content | Changed granulation, residual moisture, degradation or dissolution | **High:** Incoming checks do not cover the subsequent wetting, drying and storage exposure |
| Croscarmellose functionality and amount | Inadequate disintegration and dissolution | **High:** Model studies identify possible impairment after wet processing; identity and quantity checks do not establish processed-tablet performance[21] |
| Magnesium stearate properties and amount | Impaired dissolution or tablet integrity | **High:** Grade-dependent behaviour may interact with mixing and compression despite acceptable tablet weight and appearance[20] |

API particle size must be distinguished from processed-granule size, and incoming moisture from added water and residual granule moisture. The drying endpoint must account for the hydrate formulation; the lowest possible moisture is not automatically the most suitable endpoint.

Compatibility also requires investigation. A study of amorphous atorvastatin calcium in binary mixtures associated acidic excipient surfaces with lactone formation under accelerated storage.[19] This supports testing the complete trihydrate formulation, including calcium carbonate's intended stabilising effect, rather than assuming incompatibility or protection. Screening will also cover MCC/lactose grades, HPC and polysorbate performance, purified-water quality, and coating and packaging materials. Their omission from Table 3 does not assign Low priority. Gaps in incoming purity/potency testing would require reassessment of its conditional Medium rating.

#### 2.3 Process risks and priorities for investigation

Table 4 applies the same criteria to the selected route, assuming calibrated equipment, basic hygiene and routine tablet-weight and appearance checks in addition to the material controls above. These checks do not establish uniformity, dissolution or stability. The variables are potential critical process parameters whose significance requires investigation.[11]

**Table 4. Preliminary process risks**

| Stage and variables | Failure pathway and affected quality | Basis for High investigation priority |
|:--------------------|:-------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------|
| Initial blending: time, speed, loading | Uneven API distribution could affect dosage-unit uniformity | Correct weights do not establish mixing adequacy; later processing cannot be assumed to correct an uneven blend |
| Binder preparation and granulation: hydration, liquid quantity/addition rate, mixing exposure | Variable binder distribution or granule formation could affect feeding and dissolution | Incoming-material checks do not control changes created during granulation |
| Wet holding and drying: duration, conditions, product temperature, airflow | Moisture, degradation or solid-state changes could affect stability and dissolution; prolonged wet holding could compromise microbial limits | Processing-related form changes warrant investigation[17]; hygiene and a moisture endpoint alone do not address all chemical and microbiological pathways |
| Milling, final blending and lubrication: screen, milling conditions, mixing exposure | Granule-size changes, segregation or excessive lubrication could impair uniformity or dissolution | These effects may occur without visible defects or unacceptable tablet weight[20,22] |
| Compression: force, speed, precompression, feed settings | Inconsistent die filling or compaction could affect uniformity, integrity and dissolution | Process settings interact with granule properties; weight and appearance are incomplete indicators[22] |
| Coating: spray rate, product temperature, drying, duration | Wet/thermal exposure or unsuitable film performance could affect moisture, impurities or dissolution | An acceptable-looking immediate-release coat does not establish chemical or release performance |
| Packaging: exposure before closure, closure settings | Moisture ingress could affect stability and dissolution | Barrier material and visual inspection alone do not establish effective closure and protection[11] |

Table 4 summarises selected development concerns; omission does not establish Low risk. All seven scenarios meet the High criterion, without ranking individual variables by effect or failure frequency. Study order will follow process dependencies. After initial formulation and incoming-test review, the first three work packages will address blend/granulation consistency, wet-hold/drying effects, and milling/lubrication/feed behaviour, providing the characterised input needed for the compression experiment. Coating and packaging require their own studies. Granule size and moisture are intermediate attributes; milling settings and drying conditions are operating variables used to influence them.

#### 2.4 Development studies and proposed design-space work

Before experimentation, the team will define the quantitative formula, equipment, sampling, suitable analytical methods and response-specific criteria. Dissolution measurements must detect relevant formulation or process differences; passing dissolution alone does not establish bioequivalence. Material studies will compare representative API properties, excipient grades and amounts, compatibility and the croscarmellose addition split. Incoming purity/potency testing coverage will be verified separately.

Upstream studies will establish blend uniformity, screen binder preparation and granulation conditions, then jointly examine influential granulation and drying variables. Binder solids and added water will be distinguished to avoid unintentionally changing the formula. Stage samples will link granule moisture, size, solid form and impurities to tablets made under standardised downstream conditions. Equal endpoint moisture will not be treated as evidence of equivalent drying history or impurity profiles. Wet-hold studies will include chemical and microbiological acceptability. Milling and lubrication comparisons will assess granule size, density, flow, uniformity and tablet performance. A suitable moisture method will support drying decisions; loss on drying does not measure degradation.[12]

After feed-frame and precompression screening, the proposed compression experiment will use low, middle and high force and press-speed levels in all nine combinations, with additional independently executed centre runs. Preliminary trials will set feasible levels. This arrangement permits investigation of interaction and curvature, while repeat runs estimate experimental variability.[23] The initial study will hold formulation, granule batch, tooling and supported ancillary settings consistent. Run order will be randomised where practicable, with blocking for unavoidable day or batch differences; tablets sampled within one run are not independent process replicates.

Responses will include dissolution and dosage-unit uniformity alongside weight variation, strength, friability and disintegration. Analysis will assess effect sizes, interactions, curvature, model adequacy and uncertainty against predefined criteria. Confirmation runs will test selected combinations near proposed boundaries and across independently prepared granule batches and relevant material lots. Findings may require expanding the two-factor study. FDA's illustrative immediate-release example supports these investigation questions, but its different formulation and results are not evidence for LipidArc.[22]

Coating studies will compare cores and coated tablets for dissolution, moisture and impurities across selected coating conditions and amounts. Packaging studies will assess barrier and closure performance, including pre-sealing exposure. Long-term and accelerated studies of the packaged product will support storage and shelf-life decisions.[24]

A proposed design space must describe supported combinations, their quality effects and conditions held fixed. Separate acceptable ranges or nine passing settings alone are insufficient to establish all untested combinations. Any compression space remains conditional on justified input-granule attributes and other controls; applicability to production equipment and scale requires evidence.[11] These studies will inform parameter criticality and the controls below.

#### 2.5 Proposed control strategy

LipidArc will combine material, process and product controls using the development findings.[11] Each control will specify its method, sampling, frequency, limits, records, responsible function and response to an adverse result. In-process criteria must be supported by data and linked to finished-product specifications.[24] The proposed controls do not yet demonstrate reduced risk.

Material acceptance will cover approved sources/specifications, identity, chemical quality, relevant physical attributes and grades. Purified-water quality, storage, material status and dispensing, including API potency correction, will be controlled. Manufacturing instructions will specify the addition sequence, croscarmellose split, blending and binder preparation, granulation conditions, wet-hold limits, drying history/endpoint, milling and lubricant exposure. Development evidence will determine necessary intermediate checks, including uniformity, moisture, granule properties and any impurity or solid-form testing. Specialised development measurements will become routine tests only where their role is justified, with adequate alternative controls for any remaining concerns.

Compression will use supported force–speed combinations and feed/precompression settings. Weight, strength, thickness, friability and defect checks will monitor performance at start-up, after relevant adjustments and at justified intervals. Coating controls will cover spray/drying conditions and coating consistency; packaging controls will address pre-sealing exposure, closure integrity, labels, pack count and batch identification. Cleaning, hygiene, calibration, maintenance, line clearance and traceable handling will support all stages.

If tablets fall below an individual-weight action limit after a speed increase, production will stop compression and hold potentially affected output, initially covering material since the last satisfactory check. Investigation will examine feeding, granule flow, segregation and settings, extending the affected scope if needed. Corrective action and verification of restart conditions will precede resumed operation. Held tablets require separate quality-unit disposition; restarting does not release them. Weight checks cannot establish uniform drug distribution or dissolution.

Release will address all seven potential CQAs: identity, assay, dosage-unit uniformity, dissolution, related substances/degradation products, water content and microbial limits. QC will perform and review laboratory testing; the quality unit will review results, production/packaging records and relevant investigations before disposition. Unexplained discrepancies and specification failures require documented investigation, including potentially related batches. A later passing result alone cannot resolve an unexplained failure.[14,24]

A written stability programme will monitor relevant attributes in the intended commercial container-closure system under supported storage conditions.[24] Trends in materials, process performance, deviations, release and stability results will inform risk reassessment, corrective and preventive action and controlled changes.[10] Chapter 3 will set out the responsibilities, procedures and audit arrangements needed to maintain these controls.

### 3. QMS implementation and evaluation

#### 3.1 QMS scope, organisation and responsibilities

LipidArc's proposed QMS will translate the control strategy in Section 2.5 into assigned responsibilities and controlled activities for its 20 mg immediate-release atorvastatin tablets. The proposed system covers development and transfer into manufacture, incoming materials, wet-granulation manufacture, packaging, laboratory testing, batch disposition and ongoing stability monitoring. The assessment is FDA-focused and treats the API as an input material. Implementation will depend on completing the development, qualification and validation work needed to support routine manufacture.

Senior management will establish quality policy and objectives, provide resources and review system performance. Responsibilities, decision authority and escalation routes will be documented so that significant quality concerns reach those authorised to act.[10] LipidArc proposes a quality unit encompassing QA and QC, independent of Manufacturing in its quality decisions. QC laboratory testing forms part of this unit's wider responsibilities. The quality-unit authority required under 21 CFR §211.22 includes approval or rejection of materials and products, review of production records, and approval or rejection of procedures and specifications affecting product quality.[25]

**Table 5. Proposed functional responsibilities for QMS implementation**

| Function | Principal responsibility |
|:---------|:-------------------------|
| Development | Establish the scientific basis for controls and transfer findings, methods and unresolved risks to Manufacturing and the quality unit. |
| Manufacturing | Follow approved instructions, record operations and in-process checks, and contain and report departures. |
| QC laboratory | Perform and review testing, maintain reliable analytical data and investigate laboratory concerns. |
| Quality unit (QA and QC) | Oversee the QMS, approve relevant procedures and specifications, review investigations and batch records, and make authorised material and product disposition decisions. |
| Regulatory Affairs | Assess regulatory implications of controls and changes, including submission commitments. |
| Supporting functions | Engineering maintains equipment and utilities; purchasing and warehouse personnel implement approved sourcing, storage and material-status controls. |

For the tablet-weight excursion in Section 2.5, Manufacturing would stop compression and contain affected output. Development and Engineering would support the technical investigation, QC would provide relevant testing, and the quality unit would review the evidence and determine disposition. Recurring problems or resource needs would be escalated to management. Controlled documents and role-appropriate training will support these responsibilities and handovers, as developed in Section 3.2.

#### 3.2 Quality documentation and document control

LipidArc's quality manual will describe its QMS scope, policy, responsibilities and the relationships between quality processes, with references to supporting documents.[10] Table 6 presents the proposed structure, informed by Sanofi's historical global manual and AstraZeneca India's local documentation arrangements.[26,27] These examples guide presentation and organisation; LipidArc's content will reflect its own activities and applicable requirements.

**Table 6. Proposed quality-manual structure and supporting documents**

| Manual section | Coverage | Supporting-document examples |
|:---------------|:---------|:-----------------------------|
| Purpose and scope | Product, activities and lifecycle stages covered | Product and process overview |
| Quality policy and objectives | Quality commitments, objectives and their review | Quality objectives and performance measures |
| Organisation and responsibilities | Authority, accountability and escalation | Organisation chart and role descriptions |
| Documentation, knowledge and risk management | Document controls, knowledge transfer and risk-based decisions | Document-control SOP, development reports and risk assessments |
| Resources and operational arrangements | Personnel, facilities, equipment, materials and suppliers | Training procedures, qualification plans and supplier procedures |
| Lifecycle quality processes | Manufacturing and laboratory controls, release, stability, deviations, CAPA and changes | Manufacturing instructions, specifications and relevant SOPs |
| Evaluation and improvement | Audits, management review and improvement | Audit programme, review procedure and action records |

The manual will explain how these processes connect. Development knowledge and risk assessments inform manufacturing controls; monitoring and investigations feed CAPA and change management; audit findings and performance trends inform management review. SOPs will define recurring workflows, specifications will establish tests and acceptance criteria, and manufacturing instructions will define the approved formula, sequence and operating conditions. Protocols will set study methods and predefined criteria; completed records and reports will preserve execution, results, reviews and decisions. For example, supported drying conditions will enter manufacturing instructions, with their scientific justification retained in development or validation reports.

Each controlled document will have an identifier, version and owner, with approvals and an effective date recorded before use. Document owners will review content when changes or scheduled reviews require it. The quality unit will oversee controlled issuance and relevant approvals through a document-control SOP.[25,27] LipidArc proposes an electronic document-management system validated for its intended use, with revisions submitted as new, linked versions through review and approval. Once a revision is submitted, routine users will be prevented from overwriting or deleting it; subsequent corrections will require a new linked version. Approved versions will receive the same protection. The effective version will be clearly identified; superseded versions will be withdrawn from routine use but remain retrievable as historical records. Role-based access and secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails will preserve who performed relevant actions, what changed, when and, where appropriate, why. Audit trails will be protected from routine-user alteration and reviewed under defined, risk-based arrangements.[29]

For a proposed drying-instruction change, technical assessment and required authorisations will precede routine implementation. The owner will revise affected instructions and forms, obtain functional and quality-unit approval, arrange role-appropriate training and coordinate their effective date. Required training and any practical competency assessment will be completed before personnel independently perform affected tasks under the revised instruction, with records identifying the relevant document version. Subsequent batch records will identify the version of the instructions used and record each activity when it is performed.[28] Technical change approval establishes the acceptability of the change; document approval authorises the resulting instructions. Outcome verification is addressed in Section 3.3.[10]

If an approved instruction is found to be incorrect, the quality unit will assess the need to suspend its use or introduce authorised interim controls, identify affected batches and evaluate their quality impact through the deviation process.[24,25]

Completed records will remain legible, attributable and traceable, with corrections preserving the original entry or electronic history. Controlled access, secure storage and backup where applicable will protect them from loss or unauthorised alteration. Retention arrangements will preserve records, associated metadata and audit trails, permitting reconstruction of a batch and its document history. Disposal will require documented authorisation after applicable retention obligations have been satisfied.[27–29] Section 3.3 applies these documentation arrangements to the lifecycle quality processes.

#### 3.3 Implementation of lifecycle quality processes

LipidArc will use the following processes to maintain and revise the control strategy as development knowledge and manufacturing experience accumulate. Their formality will reflect the lifecycle stage and quality risk.[10] The examples illustrate proposed responses, not events or successful outcomes demonstrated for the product.

**Supplier and material management.** The quality unit, supported by purchasing and technical functions, will assess suppliers, establish approved material requirements and review incoming-lot performance and change notifications.[10] For magnesium stearate, the approved grade and relevant properties must remain consistent with the development evidence. Incoming lots will remain unavailable for manufacture until required sampling, testing or examination and quality-unit release are complete.[30] Supplier approval does not replace lot acceptance. Repeated discrepancies will prompt reassessment of the supplier and affected materials; a proposed grade or source change will enter change management.

**Process and product monitoring.** Manufacturing and the quality unit will review drying history, granule moisture, tablet-weight variation, release results and stability trends using defined review intervals and escalation criteria. For example, a sustained deterioration in dissolution could prompt assessment of material lots, lubrication exposure and compression conditions even while results remain within specification. An alert trend will trigger documented assessment; an action-limit or specification failure will initiate the applicable investigation and containment. Periodic product-quality review will combine these data with deviations, complaints and other relevant quality information to assess whether controls remain suitable.[10,28]

**Deviation and OOS investigation.** A departure from approved drying instructions will be recorded, potentially affected material contained, and exposure history assessed for consequences such as degradation or altered tablet performance. In a separate example, a dissolution OOS result will require prompt laboratory assessment of method execution, calculations, instrument performance and original data. If no clearly causative laboratory error is established, the quality unit will lead a full investigation covering manufacturing, sampling and potentially related batches, including distributed batches where relevant. Repeated testing cannot itself improve batch quality or replace investigation. Retesting requires scientific justification; a later passing result cannot by itself invalidate the original failure.[14] The quality unit will document the evidence supporting disposition and use the assessment of unresolved risks to determine further containment and escalation.[24,25]

**CAPA and effectiveness checks.** Actions will address investigated causes, with assigned owners, due dates, interim controls where needed and predefined effectiveness criteria.[10] If evidence identifies a feeder fault and a maintenance gap, repair would correct the immediate equipment problem; revised maintenance would address recurrence. The effectiveness review would check implementation of the revised maintenance programme and assess subsequent tablet-weight performance against the predefined criteria, including recurrence of feeder-related excursions. Completion of a repair or training session alone would not establish effectiveness. Unsuccessful actions would prompt renewed investigation and risk assessment. Batch disposition remains a separate decision from longer-term CAPA follow-up.

**Change management.** A proposed magnesium stearate grade change will be assessed by Development, Manufacturing, the quality unit and Regulatory Affairs. Comparisons will address relevant material properties, processing behaviour, dissolution and other potentially affected CQAs, with study criteria agreed before execution. The assessment will determine any stability, validation or regulatory-submission needs. Required internal approvals and applicable regulatory conditions will be satisfied before routine implementation; document revisions and training will follow Section 3.2. Post-change results will be reviewed against the predefined criteria to determine whether objectives were achieved without adverse quality effects.[10] A planned change can initiate this process without a preceding failure.

Records from these processes will supply evidence for internal audit in Section 3.4 and management review in Section 3.5.

#### 3.4 Internal audit and follow-up

LipidArc proposes a risk-based internal audit of selected controls for its atorvastatin tablets, covering incoming materials through wet-granulation manufacture, packaging and batch-release review, together with ongoing stability monitoring. Applicable US GMP requirements and approved company procedures will provide the criteria; ICH Q9(R1) and Q10 will support risk prioritisation and system evaluation.[3,10,13,31] Qualified auditors sufficiently independent of the activities examined will combine document review, site observation and staff interviews. The selection of batches and records for audit will consider Chapter 2's risks, significant changes and previous quality concerns. Auditors will document their selections and the limitations of the audit.

Table 7 names the supporting materials to be made available for each check. The twenty items are selected examples for this report and do not cover every area requiring audit; the full audit programme would address all applicable activities and requirements according to scope and risk. Items 1–5 cover facilities and equipment; 6–10 materials and manufacturing; 11–15 documentation and personnel; and 16–20 QC and quality follow-up. The listed materials describe evidence expected from a QMS in operation; LipidArc is not assumed to have generated these records or completed an audit. Records for the hypothetical changes or incidents would be requested only if such events had occurred. Blank forms specify what should be recorded; completed records and underlying data provide evidence of execution. Missing required evidence will be investigated rather than automatically treated as not applicable.

#### 3.5 Management review and continual improvement

Senior management will lead periodic review of LipidArc's pharmaceutical quality system to assess its continuing suitability and effectiveness and decide where improvement or additional resources are needed. The quality unit will coordinate inputs from Manufacturing, QC, Development, Engineering and Regulatory Affairs. Reviews will follow a documented schedule appropriate to the development and manufacturing stage; significant product-quality concerns will be escalated promptly between meetings rather than held for the next scheduled review.[10]

For the atorvastatin tablets, management will review summaries from the monitoring and product-quality reviews in Section 3.3, together with supplier performance, recurring deviations, CAPA effectiveness, change outcomes and internal-audit findings. It will also assess progress against quality objectives, outstanding actions from previous reviews and relevant regulatory developments.[10] Inputs will distinguish planned studies from completed work and emerging signals from confirmed findings.

Review decisions will set improvement priorities, allocate resources and, where justified, revise quality objectives. Each action will have a named owner, a deadline and defined evidence for assessing success. As a hypothetical example, repeated drying deviations despite completed maintenance actions would prompt management to reassess the adequacy of the investigation, corrective measures and allocated resources. Depending on the evidence, management could commission further process studies, revise the maintenance approach, fund equipment improvements or strengthen staff capability.

Minutes and an action tracker will record decisions, their rationale and follow-up responsibilities. Subsequent reviews will assess outcomes against the agreed success criteria and escalate ineffective or overdue actions. Proposed indicators include the recurrence rate of a given deviation, the number of overdue significant actions and the proportion of CAPAs meeting their effectiveness criteria; targets and assessment periods will be justified as operating experience develops. Review conclusions will be communicated to the responsible functions to inform updates to risk assessments, development knowledge and the control strategy.[10] Any resulting changes will be evaluated and implemented through the approved change-management process.

### Conclusion

The proposed approach connects the quality objectives for LipidArc’s 20 mg atorvastatin tablets to practical manufacturing and lifecycle management. The QTPP and seven potential CQAs define the intended product quality, while risk assessment directs development studies towards material and process factors that could affect it. Findings from these studies would support the specifications, manufacturing instructions and in-process controls used to produce tablets consistently.

The QMS provides the responsibilities, procedures and records needed to apply and evaluate those controls. For example, evidence from drying studies would inform approved operating conditions and monitoring requirements; batch records would document execution, and departures would trigger investigation and assessment of affected material. The quality unit would use manufacturing records, test results and relevant investigations to make batch-disposition decisions. Monitoring, CAPA, controlled changes, internal audit and management review would feed manufacturing experience back into risk assessments and the control strategy. Together, these arrangements connect scientific understanding with daily operations and continuing improvement.

This report establishes a proposed framework whose effectiveness remains to be demonstrated. Further work must define the quantitative formulation, justify specifications and operating conditions, complete qualification and validation, and provide bioequivalence and packaged-product stability evidence. The proposed design space and 24-month shelf-life remain development objectives. Successful implementation will depend on generating reliable evidence, translating it into workable controls and verifying that those controls continue to support the product’s quality objectives.
