## Report

### Introduction

#### Company description

Our company is a fictional specialty pharmaceutical company founded in 2018 and headquartered in Hong Kong, with approximately 250 employees. It focuses on cardiovascular and metabolic diseases, with core capabilities in small-molecule development and oral solid dosage forms. Its intended regulatory markets are the United States, European Union, United Kingdom and China. The company’s operating scope covers active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) and tablet manufacturing, packaging, quality-control testing and release. The company’s departments include Research and Development, Manufacturing, Regulatory Affairs, and Clinical and Safety, alongside a Quality Unit responsible for quality assurance (QA), quality control (QC) and the quality management system (QMS). Atorvastatin calcium tablets, 20 mg, are among the company’s main marketed products.

#### Product description

Our company’s generic prescription atorvastatin calcium tablets contain atorvastatin calcium trihydrate equivalent to 20 mg of atorvastatin per tablet. The product is presented as an immediate-release, film-coated oral tablet in packs of 30 tablets for home use, with or without meals. The product is intended to lower elevated blood lipids, including total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, apolipoprotein B and triglycerides. The intended population includes adults with primary hyperlipidaemia and eligible patients aged ten years and older with heterozygous or homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia, subject to the indication-specific treatment conditions.[1]

This report uses our company’s atorvastatin calcium tablets to examine how Quality by Design (QbD) and a QMS support consistent product quality in manufacturing. It defines the product’s quality objectives and potential critical quality attributes, assesses material and process risks, and proposes studies and controls to address them. It then sets out the responsibilities, documentation and review processes needed to implement those controls and evaluate their effectiveness.

### 1. Product quality foundations

#### 1.1 Applicable requirements and quality standards

This report uses US 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 as the Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) benchmark for finished-tablet manufacture. This benchmark informs the proposed controls for facilities, personnel, materials, production, laboratory testing, records and batch release.[3] ICH Q7 provides guidance for the company’s separate API-manufacturing activities.[2] The EU, UK and China remain part of the company’s wider market ambitions; the worked assessment in this report is US-focused.

Our company manufactures atorvastatin calcium API for use in its own tablets and does not market the API separately. This report focuses on finished-tablet manufacture, including the quality of the API used, without examining the API manufacturing process. For this US-focused assessment, the USP–NF monograph **Atorvastatin Calcium Tablets** provides the compendial basis for the finished product, while **Atorvastatin Calcium** provides the corresponding basis for the API entering tablet manufacture. Both monographs must be read with the applicable General Notices and general chapters.[4,5] These standards inform the tests, methods and acceptance criteria for the API and tablets, supplemented by product-specific requirements justified by development and stability evidence.

Alongside manufacturing quality controls, our company’s responsibilities for its marketed tablets include collecting and evaluating safety information and reporting adverse events in accordance with applicable requirements. These pharmacovigilance activities support continued monitoring of the product’s safety after marketing.[7] Where clinical bioequivalence studies are undertaken to support the performance target in Table 1, they should follow applicable Good Clinical Practice (GCP) standards and requirements for reliable study data.[6]

The QbD and QMS approach connects these requirements to product development and lifecycle management. ICH Q8(R2) guides the definition of product-quality targets and the understanding of formulation and process factors. ICH Q9(R1) provides the framework for assessing and managing quality risks, while ICH Q10 connects development knowledge to monitoring, corrective and preventive action (CAPA), change management and management review.[8,9] Together, these frameworks guide how our company will establish and maintain the quality of its atorvastatin tablets, alongside applicable GMP and pharmacopoeial requirements.

Dissolution is the principal product-performance attribute in this assessment. Water-content testing supports assessment of moisture-related stability and tablet performance. Studies linking water content to degradation and dissolution will establish its criticality and justify the test method and acceptance limits for our company’s tablets.[11]

### 2. QbD development and control strategy

#### 2.1 Proposed formulation and manufacturing approach

This chapter assesses wet-granulation manufacture of our company’s marketed 20 mg immediate-release atorvastatin tablets in the US FDA context. The assessment focuses on finished-tablet manufacture, beginning with atorvastatin calcium trihydrate as an input material. The selected route aims to produce granules that feed and compact consistently while supporting the tablet’s quality requirements.

The proposed tablet core contains microcrystalline cellulose (MCC) and lactose monohydrate as fillers, hydroxypropyl cellulose (HPC) as binder, croscarmellose sodium as disintegrant, polysorbate 80 as wetting agent and magnesium stearate as lubricant. Calcium carbonate is included as an alkalising component intended to support stability. This formulation and the manufacturing sequence below draw on a published atorvastatin patent as a starting point for the assessment; ingredient quantities, material grades and operating conditions require supporting evidence.[15]

The proposed manufacturing sequence begins with material acceptance, dispensing and screening, followed by blending the API, calcium carbonate, MCC, lactose and part of the croscarmellose. Separately, polysorbate 80 is incorporated into purified water, followed by HPC dispersion and hydration. This liquid is added to the initial blend during wet granulation. The granules are then dried and milled or sieved before adding the remaining croscarmellose, leaving this portion unexposed to wet granulation.[20] Magnesium stearate is added before compression, allowing its mixing exposure to be controlled separately.[21] The tablet cores then receive an immediate-release hypromellose (HPMC)-based film coat and moisture-protective packaging in the agreed 30-tablet presentation.

This formulation and manufacturing sequence provide the basis for the material and process risk assessments in Sections 2.2 and 2.3. These assessments identify factors that could affect tablet quality and prioritise them for investigation.

#### 2.2 Material risks affecting product quality

The material risk assessment prioritises investigations into API and excipient attributes that could affect tablet quality. Priorities reflect the potential consequences of failure, its qualitative likelihood and the protection provided by assumed baseline controls: identification, dispensing checks and suitable incoming chemical-quality testing. Assumptions and evidence needs are recorded to support review as knowledge develops, following ICH Q9(R1).[12]

The project defines three qualitative investigation priorities. **High** identifies a scientifically plausible, significant quality failure requiring targeted investigation and control beyond baseline checks. **Medium** identifies a consequential failure whose likelihood is expected to be reduced by a specified baseline control or supported restriction; further investigation assesses that protection and remaining uncertainty. **Low** identifies a scenario supported by evidence of a minor consequence or an unlikely failure under defined conditions. These priorities guide the allocation of investigation effort and are reassessed as supporting evidence develops.

Table 3 applies these criteria to selected material attributes and formulation factors, linking each potential quality failure to its investigation priority and supporting rationale.

**Table 3. Preliminary material and formulation risks**

| Attribute or factor | Potential quality failure | Priority and basis |
|:--------------------|:--------------------------|:-------------------|
| API particle-size distribution | Changed dissolution | **High:** Meaningful size variation is assumed possible despite acceptable identity and chemical quality |
| API solid-state form | Altered dissolution or degradation behaviour | **High:** Reported form-dependent dissolution and processing changes support investigation; incoming verification does not cover later transformation[16,17] |
| API purity and potency | Excess impurities or incorrect assay | **Medium, conditional:** Representative sampling, suitable methods and rejection are assumed to intercept unacceptable incoming API. Severity remains unchanged; later degradation is assessed separately |
| API and excipient water content | Changed granulation, residual moisture, degradation or dissolution | **High:** Incoming checks do not cover the subsequent wetting, drying and storage exposure |
| Croscarmellose functionality and amount | Inadequate disintegration and dissolution | **High:** Model studies identify possible impairment after wet processing; identity and quantity checks do not establish processed-tablet performance[20] |
| Magnesium stearate properties and amount | Impaired dissolution or tablet integrity | **High:** Grade-dependent behaviour may interact with mixing and compression despite acceptable tablet weight and appearance[19] |

The material assessment also covers factors beyond those selected in Table 3: MCC and lactose grades, HPC binder performance, polysorbate wetting performance, purified-water quality, and the suitability of coating and packaging materials. These factors extend the assessment to the functions of the remaining ingredients and the materials used to coat and protect the tablets.

Formulation compatibility is a further concern because interactions among ingredients may affect chemical stability. A study using amorphous atorvastatin calcium in binary mixtures associated acidic excipient surfaces with lactone formation under accelerated storage.[18] This finding supports assessing excipient-related degradation and calcium carbonate’s intended stabilising effect within the complete proposed formulation, with its different API solid form and combination of ingredients.

#### 2.4 Development studies and proposed design-space work

Our company will use the material and process risk assessments in Tables 3 and 4 to plan development studies linking formulation choices and manufacturing conditions to tablet quality. Each study will specify the formulation, equipment, variables, sampling plan, analytical methods and acceptance criteria.

**Material and upstream process studies**

Material studies will address the attributes and formulation factors identified in Table 3 and the accompanying discussion in Section 2.2, including API particle-size distribution, excipient grades and amounts, compatibility and the croscarmellose addition split. Dissolution testing will be designed to distinguish relevant formulation and process effects, while review of incoming purity and potency testing will assess the protection assumed in Table 3.

Studies of blending and granulation will establish suitable conditions for uniform API distribution and consistent granule formation. They will examine binder preparation, liquid addition and mixing conditions, recording incoming material moisture and added granulation water separately and controlling binder-solids content according to the study design.

Wet-hold and drying studies will assess how holding conditions and drying history affect moisture, solid-state behaviour, impurities and microbiological quality. Samples collected at relevant stages will be processed into tablets under standardised downstream conditions, linking these findings to tablet dissolution and stability. This evidence will support selection of wet-hold limits, drying conditions and a suitable moisture endpoint.[11,16]

Milling and lubrication studies will examine how mill settings and lubricant-mixing exposure affect granule-size distribution, density, flow, blend uniformity and tablet performance. The findings will help define suitable granule attributes and preparation conditions for the subsequent compression studies.

**Compression experiment**

The compression experiment provides the detailed example of experimental design in this chapter. Preliminary trials will assess feed-frame and precompression settings and identify feasible ranges of main compression force and press speed for the selected formulation, equipment and tooling. Three numerical levels for each factor will then be selected and documented in the study protocol, with their justification based on these trials. The main experiment will evaluate all nine force–speed combinations, with additional independently executed runs at the centre combination to assess repeatability.[22]

The initial experiment will use a consistent formulation, granule batch, tooling and ancillary settings to support comparison between force–speed combinations. Run order will be randomised where practicable, and unavoidable day or batch differences will be accounted for through blocking. Sampling within each run will characterise tablet variability, while independently repeated runs will assess variation between experimental executions.

The compression study will measure dissolution and dosage-unit uniformity, together with tablet weight variation, strength, friability and disintegration. Analysis will examine how strongly compression force and press speed affect these results, whether the effect of one factor depends on the other, and whether the relationships are nonlinear. The analysis will also assess how well the fitted models describe the observations and the uncertainty in their predictions, using predefined criteria.[21,22]

Confirmation runs will assess whether the selected force–speed combinations produce consistent tablet quality, including combinations near the boundaries of the proposed operating region. These runs will use independently prepared granule batches and relevant material lots to examine performance under material variability. Where the findings identify additional influential factors, the experimental design will be expanded to investigate them.

**Coating, packaging and stability studies**

Coating studies will compare cores and coated tablets for dissolution, moisture and impurities across selected coating conditions and amounts.

Packaging studies will assess barrier and closure performance, including pre-sealing exposure. Long-term and accelerated studies of the packaged product will support storage and shelf-life decisions.[23]

**Proposed compression design space**

These studies will support evaluation of a proposed compression design space linking force–speed combinations to tablet quality under defined material and operating conditions.[10] The combined findings will inform the control strategy in Section 2.5.

#### 2.5 Proposed control strategy

Our company will combine material, process and product controls using the development findings.[10] Each control will specify its method, sampling, frequency, limits, records, responsible function and response to an adverse result. In-process criteria will be supported by data and linked to finished-product specifications.[23] Together, these controls will be designed to maintain consistent tablet quality and enable timely detection and response to deviations.

Our company will check that incoming materials come from approved sources and meet the required specifications for identity, purity, potency, physical properties and grade. Procedures will define how materials are stored, approved for use and weighed for production. The amount of API weighed will account for its measured potency, and purified water will meet the quality requirements for its intended use.

Manufacturing instructions will explain when and how to add each ingredient, including the two additions of croscarmellose. They will also specify the conditions for blending, binder preparation, granulation, wet holding, drying, milling and lubricant mixing.

Routine checks will monitor the material and intermediate-product attributes selected through development, such as blend uniformity, moisture, granule properties, impurities or solid form.

Routine production will follow the compression, coating and packaging conditions established through the studies in Section 2.4. During compression, operators will check tablet weight, strength, thickness, friability and visible defects at start-up, after relevant adjustments and at intervals defined in the manufacturing instructions. Coating and packaging checks will confirm coating consistency, closure integrity, correct labels, pack count and batch identification.

Cleaning, hygiene, equipment calibration and maintenance will support these operations. Line-clearance checks will confirm that materials and documents from previous production have been removed, while handling records will maintain traceability.

Deviations identified through routine controls will be investigated to establish their causes and assess their effect on product quality. The CAPA system will use these findings to address identified causes, reduce the risk of recurrence and check whether the actions are effective.[9]

For example, if individual tablet weights fall below the action limit after a press-speed increase, operators will stop compression and hold potentially affected tablets, initially covering production since the last satisfactory check. The investigation will examine feeding, granule flow, segregation and operating settings, and determine whether additional output is affected. Findings will guide corrective measures, with suitable operating conditions verified before compression resumes. The quality unit will decide the disposition of held tablets using the investigation findings and relevant quality tests. Follow-up monitoring will assess whether the actions prevent further weight excursions.

Batch release will confirm that the finished tablets meet the specifications developed from the quality attributes in Table 2. QC will perform and review the required laboratory testing. The quality unit will assess these results together with production and packaging records and relevant investigation findings to decide whether the batch is suitable for release.[23]

An ongoing stability programme will monitor whether tablets in the selected moisture-protective packaging continue to meet the relevant quality criteria throughout their shelf life.[23] Chapter 3 will set out the responsibilities, procedures and audit arrangements needed to maintain these controls.

### 3. QMS implementation and evaluation

#### 3.1 QMS scope, organisation and responsibilities

Our company's proposed QMS will translate the control strategy in Section 2.5 into assigned responsibilities and controlled activities for its 20 mg immediate-release atorvastatin tablets. The proposed system covers development and transfer into manufacture, incoming materials, wet-granulation manufacture, packaging, laboratory testing, batch disposition and ongoing stability monitoring. Implementation will be supported by the relevant development, qualification and validation evidence.

The quality-unit authority required under 21 CFR §211.22 includes approval or rejection of materials and products, review of production records, and approval or rejection of procedures and specifications affecting product quality.[24] Company management will establish quality policy and objectives, provide resources and review system performance. Responsibilities, decision authority and escalation routes will be documented so that significant quality concerns reach those authorised to act.[9]

#### 3.3 Implementation of lifecycle quality processes

Our company will maintain and improve the control strategy through supplier and material management, process and product monitoring, deviation and out-of-specification (OOS) investigation, CAPA, and change management. These processes will connect routine oversight with investigation, corrective action and controlled improvements, using the documentation arrangements described in Section 3.2.[9] The following examples show how these processes would apply to our company's atorvastatin tablets.

**Supplier and material management.** The quality unit, supported by purchasing and technical functions, will assess suppliers, establish approved material requirements and review incoming-lot performance and change notifications.[9] For magnesium stearate, the approved grade and relevant properties must remain consistent with the development evidence. Supplier approval establishes the suitability of the source. The quality unit will release each incoming lot for manufacture after required sampling, testing or examination confirms that it meets the approved material requirements.[29] Repeated discrepancies will prompt reassessment of the supplier and affected materials; a proposed grade or source change will enter change management.

**Process and product monitoring.** Manufacturing and the quality unit will review drying history, granule moisture, tablet-weight variation, release results and stability trends using defined review intervals and escalation criteria. For example, a sustained deterioration in dissolution could prompt assessment of material lots, lubrication exposure and compression conditions even while results remain within specification. An alert trend will trigger documented assessment; an action-limit or specification failure will initiate the applicable investigation and containment.

Product-quality complaints will provide additional feedback on the quality of supplied tablets. Periodic product-quality review will bring this feedback together with routine monitoring results and deviation-investigation findings to assess whether controls remain suitable.[9,27]

**Deviation and OOS investigation.** For example, a departure from approved drying instructions will be recorded, potentially affected material contained, and exposure history assessed for consequences such as degradation or altered tablet performance.

For a dissolution OOS result, the laboratory will assess method execution, calculations, instrument performance and original data. When the cause remains unresolved after laboratory assessment, the quality unit will lead a full investigation covering manufacturing, sampling and potentially related batches, including distributed batches where relevant. The investigation will assess batch quality using the original results and any scientifically justified retesting. Invalidation of the original OOS result will require documented scientific evidence explaining the result.[13] The quality unit will document the evidence supporting disposition and use the assessment of unresolved risks to determine further containment and escalation.[23,24]

**CAPA and effectiveness checks.** Actions will address investigated causes, with assigned owners, due dates, interim controls where needed and predefined effectiveness criteria.[9] For example, if evidence identifies a feeder fault and a maintenance gap, repair would correct the immediate equipment problem and revised maintenance would address recurrence. Follow-up would check implementation of the revised maintenance programme and assess subsequent tablet-weight performance against the predefined effectiveness criteria, including recurrence of feeder-related excursions, with unsuccessful actions prompting renewed investigation and risk assessment. During this longer-term follow-up, batch disposition would be determined through the investigation and quality assessment described above.

**Change management.** Change management will provide a structured process for assessing, approving, implementing and reviewing changes to materials, processes and controls. It will support planned improvements and changes arising from investigations or CAPA, with technical and regulatory assessment guiding implementation and follow-up.[9]

For example, a proposed magnesium stearate grade change will be assessed by Development, Manufacturing, the quality unit and Regulatory Affairs. Comparisons will address relevant material properties, processing behaviour, dissolution and other potentially affected CQAs, with study criteria agreed before execution. The assessment will determine any stability, validation or regulatory-submission needs. Before routine implementation, the company will obtain required internal approvals, satisfy applicable regulatory conditions, update controlled documents and complete the relevant training. Post-change results will be reviewed against the predefined criteria to assess achievement of the intended objectives and maintenance of product quality.[9]

Records from these processes will supply evidence for internal audit in Section 3.4 and management review in Section 3.5.

#### 3.4 Internal audit and follow-up

Our company proposes a risk-based internal audit of selected controls for its atorvastatin tablets, covering incoming materials through wet-granulation manufacture, packaging and batch-release review, together with ongoing stability monitoring. Applicable US GMP requirements and approved company procedures will provide the criteria; ICH Q9(R1) and Q10 will support risk prioritisation and system evaluation.[3,9,12,30] Qualified auditors sufficiently independent of the activities examined will combine document review, site observation and staff interviews. The selection of batches and records for audit will consider the risks discussed in Chapter 2, significant changes, and findings from the monitoring and investigation processes described in Section 3.3. Auditors will document their selections and the limitations of the audit.

#### 3.5 Management review and continual improvement

Company management will lead periodic review of our company's pharmaceutical quality system to assess its continuing suitability and effectiveness and decide where improvement or additional resources are needed. The quality unit will coordinate inputs from Manufacturing, QC, Development, Engineering and Regulatory Affairs. Reviews will follow a documented schedule appropriate to the development and manufacturing stage; significant product-quality concerns will be escalated promptly between meetings rather than held for the next scheduled review.[9]

For the atorvastatin tablets, management will review the monitoring and product-quality findings described in Section 3.3, together with supplier performance, recurring deviations, CAPA effectiveness, the results of implemented changes and internal-audit findings. It will also review progress towards quality objectives, unfinished actions from previous reviews and relevant regulatory developments.[9] The review materials will clearly distinguish planned studies from completed work and possible quality concerns from confirmed problems.

Management will use these findings to set improvement priorities, assign the necessary resources and revise quality objectives where appropriate. Each agreed action will have a responsible person, a deadline and clear criteria for judging whether it worked. For example, if drying deviations continue after maintenance actions are completed, management will review the investigation, the actions taken and the resources provided. Based on the findings, it may request further process studies, revise maintenance procedures, fund equipment improvements or provide additional staff training.

Meeting minutes will record management’s decisions and the reasons for them. An action tracker will list the agreed tasks, responsible people, deadlines and progress. At later reviews, management will check whether each action achieved its intended result and decide what further steps are needed for overdue or ineffective actions.

To assess progress, management will track how often the same deviation recurs, how many important actions are overdue and what proportion of CAPAs meet their agreed effectiveness criteria. Targets and review periods will be set using operating experience. The findings will be shared with the responsible departments to update risk assessments, understanding of the product and process, and manufacturing controls.[9] Any resulting changes will follow the approved change-management process.

### Conclusion

This report proposes a framework connecting the quality objectives for our company’s 20 mg atorvastatin tablets with manufacturing and lifecycle management. Applying this framework requires evidence supporting the quantitative formulation, specifications, operating conditions, qualification and validation, bioequivalence and stability in the selected packaging. The proposed compression design space and 24-month shelf-life target require supporting study results. This evidence provides the basis for selecting suitable controls and assessing their effectiveness.

The QTPP and seven potential CQAs define the intended product quality, while risk assessment directs development studies towards material and process factors that could affect it. Findings from these studies would support the specifications, manufacturing instructions and in-process controls used to produce tablets consistently.

The QMS defines who is responsible for applying these controls, which procedures they follow and what records they keep. For example, drying studies would support operating conditions and monitoring requirements. Batch records would document how drying was performed, while deviations would trigger investigation and assessment of affected material. The quality unit would review manufacturing records, test results and investigations to decide whether affected batches can be released. Monitoring, CAPA, change management, internal audit and management review would use manufacturing experience to update risk assessments and improve controls. Together, these arrangements connect scientific understanding with daily operations and continuing improvement.
