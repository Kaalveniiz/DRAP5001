1. **Drug substance:** atorvastatin calcium active pharmaceutical ingredient, manufactured internally.
2. **Drug product:** an immediate-release, film-coated oral tablet containing the internally manufactured active ingredient.

Current working profile:

- Product: Investigational Atorvastatin Calcium Tablets

#### GLP — applicability assessment

**Decision for this case:** **Yes, during the nonclinical safety stage, provided the company performs or commissions regulated nonclinical safety studies to support its clinical-trial applications.** GLP does not govern API synthesis, tablet manufacture, routine quality-control testing, or every exploratory laboratory experiment.

**Activity that triggers GLP:** The virtual company generates nonclinical safety data for submission in support of the US IND, EU clinical-trial application, or Chinese clinical-trial application. The exact new studies needed cannot yet be specified because the investigational difference from existing atorvastatin products has not been defined. Existing knowledge may be usable, while a sufficiently different formulation, exposure, route, or indication may create a need for additional studies.

**What GLP controls:** The organisation and conditions under which regulated nonclinical safety studies are planned, performed, monitored, recorded, reported, and archived. It is a study-quality and data-integrity system, not the scientific list of studies that must be conducted.

**Applicable framework:**

- **International baseline:** [OECD Principles on Good Laboratory Practice](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-principles-on-good-laboratory-practice_9789264078536-en.html).
- **Scientific programme:** [ICH M3(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/M3_R2__Guideline.pdf) guides the type and timing of nonclinical safety studies supporting human trials and marketing applications. ICH M3(R2) is not itself the GLP quality-system standard.
- **United States:** FDA [21 CFR Part 58](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/inspection-references/nonclinical-laboratories-inspected-under-good-laboratory-practices) for nonclinical laboratory studies supporting applications for research or marketing permits.
- **European Union:** [Directive 2004/10/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A02004L0010-20090420) applies the OECD GLP principles; [Directive 2004/9/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2004/9/2019-07-26/eng) covers inspection and verification of GLP compliance.
- **China:** [Good Laboratory Practice for Nonclinical Drug Research, 2017 revision](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_2959b53d3b6a429e866c514a76a790db.html), CFDA Order No. 34, effective 1 September 2017, for nonclinical safety-evaluation studies conducted for drug registration.

**Shortest accurate working line:**

> **GLP — Applicable during nonclinical safety development:** regulated nonclinical safety studies supporting clinical-trial applications must comply with applicable GLP requirements; OECD GLP provides the international baseline, supplemented by US 21 CFR Part 58, EU Directives 2004/10/EC and 2004/9/EC, and China's 2017 drug GLP.

**Confidence:** High for the scope and named standards; conditional for the exact atorvastatin studies required because the investigational change is not yet defined.

#### GCP — applicability assessment

**Decision for this case:** **Yes. GCP applies when the investigational atorvastatin tablet is administered to human trial participants.** It applies to the company's interventional clinical trials intended to support regulatory submissions, including Phase I, II, and III; it is not restricted to Phase II/III.

**Activity that triggers GCP:** The virtual company acts as the sponsor of clinical trials in the United States, European Union, and China. The company must ensure that trial design, ethics review, informed consent, investigator and site selection, investigational-product accountability, monitoring, data handling, safety reporting, analysis, and reporting meet GCP and regional requirements. Delegating work to a CRO does not remove the sponsor's responsibility for oversight.

**What GCP controls:** The ethical, scientific, and quality standard for planning, initiating, conducting, recording, overseeing, evaluating, analysing, and reporting trials involving human participants. Its central purposes are protection of participants' rights, safety, and well-being and production of reliable clinical-trial results.

**Applicable framework:**

- **International baseline:** [ICH E6(R3) Principles and Annex 1](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6%28R3%29_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106_ErrorCorrections_2025_1024.pdf). Annex 1 is the relevant annex for an ordinary interventional atorvastatin trial. ICH E6(R3) should be read with applicable regional law; it does not replace regional authorisation and legal requirements.
- **United States:** [FDA E6(R3) Good Clinical Practice, final guidance issued September 2025](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e6r3-good-clinical-practice-gcp), together with the binding FDA framework, principally 21 CFR Part 50 on protection and informed consent of human participants, Part 56 on institutional review boards, and Part 312 on INDs and sponsor/investigator responsibilities. Part 54 may apply to clinical-investigator financial disclosure, and Part 11 may apply to electronic records and signatures.
- **European Union:** [ICH E6(R3) Principles and Annex 1 became effective in the EU on 23 July 2025](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/ich-e6-good-clinical-practice-scientific-guideline), together with [Regulation (EU) No 536/2014](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/clinical-trials/clinical-trials-regulation-eu-no-5362014_en) and applicable Member State requirements. The Regulation governs clinical-trial authorisation, participant protection, informed consent, conduct, safety reporting, and transparency in the EU.
- **China:** [Good Clinical Practice for Drug Trials, 2026 revision](https://yjj.sh.gov.cn/qtgzwj/20260608/89cbf084b5cb4eab8fd0fb56d8538a1d.html), NMPA Announcement No. 50 of 2026, effective 1 September 2026. It replaced the 2020 Chinese GCP and governs clinical-trial quality, participant protection, data reliability, ethics, and responsibilities of sponsors, investigators, and trial institutions.

**Shortest accurate working line:**

> **GCP — Applicable to human clinical trials:** conduct the atorvastatin trials under ICH E6(R3) Principles and Annex 1, together with US 21 CFR Parts 50, 56, and 312, EU Regulation 536/2014, and China's 2026 drug GCP.

**Confidence:** High for applicability and the named current frameworks as of 2026-09-08.

#### GMP — applicability assessment

**Decision for this case:** **Yes; GMP is fully applicable and is the central GxP area for the virtual company.** It applies because the company manufactures both the atorvastatin calcium active pharmaceutical ingredient and the finished investigational tablet. GMP obligations begin before human dosing and continue through clinical and commercial manufacture.

**Activities that trigger GMP:**

1. Synthesis, isolation, purification, physical processing, testing, packaging, storage, and release of the atorvastatin calcium API.
2. Receipt and control of API, excipients, and packaging materials for the tablet.
3. Manufacture, in-process control, packaging, labelling, testing, stability assessment, storage, and batch release of the investigational tablet.
4. Manufacture of later clinical and commercial batches as the process and control strategy mature.

**What GMP controls:** The pharmaceutical quality system; independent quality oversight; trained personnel; suitable premises, utilities, and equipment; material and supplier controls; approved procedures and contemporaneous records; production and in-process controls; laboratory controls and stability work; qualification and validation; deviation, out-of-specification, CAPA, and change-control systems; data integrity; batch review and release; complaints, recalls, self-inspection, and outsourced activities where applicable.

**Two GMP scopes must remain separate:**

- **Drug substance/API:** ICH Q7 is the harmonised GMP guideline. Section 19 specifically addresses APIs manufactured for use in clinical trials.
- **Finished investigational tablet:** ICH Q7 does not govern manufacture of the finished dosage form. The applicable investigational-product and finished-product GMP framework must be identified separately for each region and development phase.

**Applicable framework:**

- **International API baseline:** [ICH Q7, Good Manufacturing Practice Guide for Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/Q7%20Guideline.pdf), including Section 19 for APIs used in clinical trials. There is no single ICH document that functions as a complete global GMP code for finished pharmaceutical products.
- **United States:** The atorvastatin API is covered by FDA's [ICH Q7 guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/q7a-good-manufacturing-practice-guidance-active-pharmaceutical-ingredients). Finished drugs are generally governed by 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211. Most Phase I investigational drugs remain subject to statutory CGMP but are exempt from Part 211 under 21 CFR 210.2(c); FDA's [CGMP for Phase 1 Investigational Drugs](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/current-good-manufacturing-practice-phase-1-investigational-drugs) provides the phase-appropriate framework. The Part 211 exemption ends in the circumstances specified in 21 CFR 210.2(c), including use in Phase II or III.
- **European Union:** [EudraLex Volume 4](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/eudralex/eudralex-volume-4_en) supplies the EU GMP framework. Part II addresses active substances used as starting materials. Manufacture of the finished investigational tablet is governed by the EU requirements for investigational medicinal products, including Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/1569 and the detailed guideline listed as Annex 13; relevant Part I principles and other applicable annexes also need to be considered.
- **China:** [Good Manufacturing Practice for Drugs, 2010 revision](https://english.nmpa.gov.cn/2019-07/25/c_390613.htm) supplies the general drug-manufacturing quality framework. [NMPA Announcement No. 43 of 2022 and its Annex on Investigational Medicinal Products](https://cfdi.org.cn/cfdi/resource/news/14654.html), effective 1 July 2022, add requirements specifically for investigational products. Chinese manufacturing and registration law remains controlling in addition to any ICH guidance used as a technical baseline.

**Phase-appropriate point:** "Phase-appropriate" means that the extent of process knowledge, validation, specifications, and supporting documentation develops as clinical development advances. It does not mean that early clinical material may be made without a quality system, controlled manufacture, testing, documentation, and batch-release decision.

**Shortest accurate working line:**

> **GMP — Fully applicable and primary:** apply ICH Q7 to in-house atorvastatin calcium API manufacture and the applicable US, EU, and Chinese investigational-product GMP requirements to manufacture and release the finished clinical tablet.

**Confidence:** High for applicability, the separation between API and finished-product GMP, and the named current frameworks as of 2026-09-08.

#### GVP / pharmacovigilance — applicability assessment

**Meaning:** **GVP means Good Pharmacovigilance Practice.** Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities concerned with detecting, assessing, understanding, and preventing adverse effects or other medicine-related problems. Unlike GMP, it does not control how a batch is manufactured; it controls how safety information is collected, evaluated, reported, and acted upon across the product lifecycle.

**Decision for this case:** **Yes, pharmacovigilance is applicable, but the applicable framework is stage-specific.** Because the atorvastatin tablet is presently an investigational product, clinical-trial safety surveillance and reporting apply now. A full post-authorisation pharmacovigilance system becomes relevant if and when the product is approved and marketed. It would therefore be misleading to describe the present IND-stage obligation only as “post-market safety and RMP.”

**During clinical development:** The sponsor must collect and evaluate adverse events and serious adverse events, assess suspected adverse reactions, submit expedited safety reports when the relevant criteria are met, review accumulating safety information, and submit periodic or annual development-safety reports as required. The principal harmonised references are [ICH E2A, Clinical Safety Data Management: Definitions and Standards for Expedited Reporting](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E2A_Guideline.pdf) and [ICH E2F, Development Safety Update Report](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E2F_Guideline.pdf), together with GCP and each region's clinical-trial legislation.

**After marketing authorisation:** The company must operate continuing safety surveillance, including collection and assessment of suspected adverse reactions, signal management, periodic benefit-risk evaluation, risk minimisation, regulatory reporting, and maintenance of the pharmacovigilance system. [ICH E2E, Pharmacovigilance Planning](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E2E_Guideline.pdf) provides a harmonised planning framework, while the binding and operational requirements remain region-specific.

**Applicable regional framework:**

- **United States:** During the IND stage, sponsor safety reporting is principally governed by [21 CFR 312.32](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-312/subpart-B/section-312.32), with annual reports under [21 CFR 312.33](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-312/subpart-B/section-312.33). After approval, postmarketing adverse-drug-experience reporting is governed principally by [21 CFR 314.80](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-314/subpart-B/section-314.80). The United States does not use one single binding code titled “GVP” equivalent to the EU modular GVP framework.
- **European Union:** Clinical-trial safety reporting is governed by [Regulation (EU) No 536/2014](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/clinical-trials/clinical-trials-regulation-eu-no-5362014_en); sponsors report relevant suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions and other required safety information through the EU clinical-trial safety system. The [EMA Good Pharmacovigilance Practices](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/pharmacovigilance-post-authorisation/good-pharmacovigilance-practices-gvp) modules principally govern the post-authorisation pharmacovigilance system. An EU risk-management plan is one part of that framework, not the entire meaning of GVP.
- **China:** Clinical-trial safety is governed by China's current GCP and associated drug-trial reporting requirements. China's [Good Pharmacovigilance Practice](https://english.nmpa.gov.cn/2021-05/13/c_655095.htm), effective 1 December 2021, applies to drug-registration applicants as well as marketing-authorisation holders, so pharmacovigilance responsibilities are not limited to the post-marketing period.

**Shortest accurate working line:**

> **GVP / pharmacovigilance — Applicable, stage-specific:** manage investigational safety under ICH E2A and E2F plus US 21 CFR Part 312, EU Regulation 536/2014, and Chinese clinical-trial requirements; after approval, operate the required regional pharmacovigilance system, including EU GVP, US 21 CFR 314.80, and China's GVP.

**Confidence:** High for applicability, the distinction between development and post-authorisation pharmacovigilance, and the named core frameworks as of 2026-09-08.

### Detailed explanation and fact-check

> **Fact-check date:** 2026-09-08  
> **Working scope:** A 20 mg immediate-release, film-coated atorvastatin tablet for oral administration.

[ICH Q8(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/Q8_R2_Guideline.pdf) defines the QTPP as a prospective summary of the quality characteristics of a drug product that should ideally be achieved to ensure the desired quality, taking safety and efficacy into account. It commonly includes dosage form, route of administration, strength, drug-release or delivery characteristics, container-closure system and quality criteria appropriate to the product.

The additional **Related CQA(s)** column connects each product target directly to the following CQA slide. Test methods, final numerical acceptance criteria, material attributes and process parameters are deliberately excluded because they are established later through development, risk assessment and the control strategy. The 24-month shelf-life is an initial development target rather than an already demonstrated claim; it must be confirmed by an appropriate stability programme.

## Slide 6: Potential CQAs

### Content for the slide

| Category | Potential CQA | Why Critical |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity and strength** | Identification and assay | Confirms that the tablet contains atorvastatin at the correct strength. |
| **Dose consistency** | Uniformity of dosage units | Ensures that every tablet delivers a consistent 20 mg dose. |
| **Product performance** | Dissolution | Ensures consistent drug release and supports reliable absorption. |
| **Purity and stability** | Related substances / degradation products | Controls potentially harmful impurities and product deterioration during storage. |
| **Microbiological quality** | Microbial limits | Controls microbial contamination in the non-sterile oral tablet. |

**Key point:** These attributes determine whether each atorvastatin tablet has the correct identity, dose, purity and drug-release performance throughout its shelf-life.

### Detailed explanation and fact-check

> **Fact-check date:** 2026-09-08  
> **Working scope:** The finished 20 mg immediate-release, film-coated atorvastatin tablet.

[ICH Q8(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/Q8_R2_Guideline.pdf) defines a CQA as a physical, chemical, biological or microbiological property that must remain within an appropriate limit, range or distribution to ensure product quality. For solid oral dosage forms, the typical concerns are purity, strength, drug release and stability. The list above is therefore a list of **potential** CQAs; it should later be prioritised through quality-risk assessment and development evidence.

- **Identity and assay:** Identification confirms that the correct active ingredient is present; assay confirms that its amount corresponds to the intended strength.
- **Uniformity of dosage units:** Each tablet must deliver a consistent dose rather than only achieving the correct average batch assay.
- **Dissolution:** [ICH Q6A](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/Q6A%20Guideline.pdf) states that specifications for solid oral dosage forms normally include a drug-release test. Dissolution is particularly relevant because atorvastatin calcium has limited aqueous solubility, including insolubility at pH 4 and below, as described in the official [DailyMed product information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=4636ca02-79c3-5a35-e054-00144ff8d46c).
- **Related substances and degradation products:** These must be controlled to protect purity, safety and stability throughout shelf-life.
- **Microbial limits:** This is a non-sterile oral tablet, so microbial quality must be controlled, but sterility and endotoxin testing are not ordinary CQAs for this dosage form.

## Slide 7: Initial Quality Risk Assessment

### Content for the slide

| CQA | What Might Go Wrong? | Likelihood | Severity | Initial Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Identification and assay** | Wrong API or strength from mix-up, potency variation, dispensing error or processing loss | **Medium** | **High** | **High** |
| **Uniformity of dosage units** | Dose variation from poor flow, inadequate mixing, segregation or inconsistent die filling | **Medium** | **High** | **High** |
| **Dissolution** | Slow or variable release from API form or particle size, excipients, lubrication or compression | **High** | **High** | **High** |
| **Related substances / degradation products** | Excess impurities from input materials, heat, moisture, oxygen or unsuitable packaging | **Medium** | **High** | **High** |
| **Microbial limits** | Excess bioburden from raw materials, the environment, handling or poor hygiene | **Low** | **High** | **Medium** |

*Preliminary qualitative ratings assume baseline GMP controls and must be reviewed when product-specific data become available.*

**Key point:** Dissolution has the highest initial likelihood. Dose uniformity, correct strength and impurity control also require high-priority development and control.

### Detailed explanation and fact-check

> **Fact-check date:** 2026-09-08  
> **Working scope:** Preliminary risk assessment for the finished 20 mg immediate-release atorvastatin tablet before formulation and process optimisation.

**Risk question:** What material or processing failures could cause a finished-tablet CQA to fall outside its acceptable range and thereby affect patient safety, efficacy or product quality?

[ICH Q9(R1)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_Q9%28R1%29_Guideline_Step4_2022_1219.pdf) describes risk as a combination of the probability of harm and the severity of that harm. Its three fundamental risk-assessment questions are: what might go wrong, how likely is it to go wrong, and what would the consequences be? It permits qualitative descriptors such as high, medium and low when they are defined and the assumptions and uncertainty are made visible.

- **High likelihood:** The failure is strongly plausible without targeted formulation or process understanding and control.
- **Medium likelihood:** The failure is reasonably plausible because several material or process variables can cause it.
- **Low likelihood:** The failure is less likely for a dry oral tablet manufactured under normal GMP controls, although it remains possible.
- **High severity:** Failure could materially affect dose, safety, efficacy or product performance.
- **High initial risk:** High severity combined with medium or high likelihood.
- **Medium initial risk:** High severity combined with low likelihood.

The ratings are an **initial qualitative risk ranking**, not a calculated FMEA risk-priority number. They assume that the company follows baseline GMP but has not yet established product-specific formulation and process controls. The ratings must be reviewed when formulation composition, manufacturing process, experimental results, deviations and stability data become available, as required by the lifecycle approach in ICH Q9(R1).
